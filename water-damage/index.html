Two Soil Zones, Two Different Failure Patterns

Round Rock is unusual in that it straddles two distinct soil zones, and which side of town a property sits on changes how water damage typically shows up. The west half rests on Hill Country limestone, a porous karst where a leak can travel sideways underground and surface well away from its actual source. The east half sits on Blackland Prairie clay, which swells after rain and shrinks hard during drought, a constant motion that stresses slab plumbing and sewer laterals until a joint finally gives.

What Dell-Era Neighborhoods Are Dealing With Now

Forest Creek, Brushy Creek, and Cat Hollow went up in the late 1980s and 1990s during the boom that followed Dell setting up headquarters nearby. The original copper plumbing in those homes is now thirty to forty years old, squarely inside the window where pinhole leaks start becoming common. Newer developments like Teravista, Paloma Lake, and Mayfield Ranch run on PEX, which resists mineral buildup but still fails at fittings and connection points over time.

Extraction Starts the Same Visit the Source Is Found

Once the source is identified, extraction and structural drying begin the same visit rather than getting scheduled separately. Commercial air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, and moisture readings get checked daily in framing, subfloor, and drywall until the numbers confirm the space is actually dry, not just dry to the touch.

Why Round Rock's Hard Water Adds to the Picture

Since the city's supply shifted to Lake Travis through the Brushy Creek Regional Utility Authority partnership in June 2020, testing has run near 15.2 grains per gallon. That mineral load builds scale inside copper supply lines and works against the metal from the inside, which is part of why pinhole failures cluster in homes with original plumbing from the 1990s.

Classifying the Water Before Deciding What's Salvageable

A clean supply line break is Category 1 and dries out without much drama. Once water has picked up contamination, from a sewer backup, from floodwater, from anything outside the clean plumbing system, it becomes Category 2 or 3 and needs actual disinfection before drying can start. That classification happens during the first visit, since it determines what gets dried in place versus what has to be removed entirely.

Why a Phone Estimate Only Goes So Far

A stained ceiling looks the same on the phone whether it's a minor drip or a much bigger problem hiding above it, and there's no way to tell the difference without actually seeing the space. We can give you a ballpark for context, but the real number comes after someone's had eyes on it directly.

What to Expect During the First Visit

A technician arrives with moisture meters and, where the source isn't obvious, thermal imaging to trace where water has actually traveled. That visit typically confirms the source, starts initial extraction if water is still present, and leaves you with a written scope before equipment runs overnight unattended.

Why Pricing Gets Scoped Before Work Begins

Every job gets a written scope before drying equipment goes in, covering what's being dried, what's being removed, and roughly what timeline to expect. That upfront scope protects you from surprise costs and gives your insurance adjuster something concrete to review.

Water Damage Restoration Questions From Round Rock Homeowners

What's realistic for how quickly you can get out here?

For active water, we prioritize same-day dispatch across Round Rock and the surrounding Williamson County cities we cover. A slow, already-stopped drip can usually be scheduled within normal business hours instead.

Does it matter which side of town my house is on?

It genuinely does. Homes on the west side's limestone karst can have a leak surface well away from its actual source, while homes on the east side's Blackland clay tend to see more stress-related joint failures from the soil's constant swelling and shrinking.

Will my homeowner's insurance cover this?

Sudden and accidental water damage, a burst line or a failed appliance, is typically covered under a standard homeowners policy. Flood damage from rising water like Brushy Creek overflow usually requires separate flood insurance, and we're glad to help you understand which category your situation falls into.
